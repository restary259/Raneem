# Public copy tone cleanup

## Goal
Replace awkward marketing phrases built around “real,” “authentic,” or defensive promises with clear, specific language that sounds professional in English, Arabic, and Hebrew.

## Changes
- Replace the homepage destination heading:
  - EN: **Study destinations across Germany**
  - AR: **وجهات للدراسة في ألمانيا**
  - HE: **יעדי לימוד ברחבי גרמניה**
- Replace the homepage trust heading:
  - EN: **Professional guidance. Clear responsibilities.**
  - AR: **إرشاد مهني ومسؤوليات واضحة**
  - HE: **ליווי מקצועי. אחריות ברורה.**
- Replace the Contact-page label “A REAL PERSON, WHEN YOU NEED ONE” and its Arabic/Hebrew equivalents with a direct, human phrase such as **Speak directly with our team**, translated naturally rather than literally.
- Replace other public promotional uses where “real” is filler rather than useful information, including the partnership success subtitle and resource-card titles/descriptions. Prefer concrete wording such as **partner outcomes**, **practical arrival guide**, and **living costs by city**.
- Keep legitimate uses that mean something different, including **real-time**, legally necessary no-guarantee language, and ordinary prose such as “a real challenge.”

## Copy standards
- Say what the section contains instead of claiming it is “real.”
- Keep legal accuracy, but place formal disclaimers in body/legal copy rather than turning them into awkward marketing headlines.
- Avoid unverifiable superlatives, vague trust claims, and literal translations.
- Preserve the existing page structure, links, imagery, and actions; this is a wording-only cleanup.

## Technical details
- Update both bundled and public locale copies where the project maintains duplicates.
- Keep English, Arabic, and Hebrew meaning aligned while using natural phrasing in each language.
- Re-scan public locale files for the targeted wording after edits, run locale-parity/title-punctuation tests, and visually verify the affected homepage, Contact, Partnership, and Resources sections in all three languages.
