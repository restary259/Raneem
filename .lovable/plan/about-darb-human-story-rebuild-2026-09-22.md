# About DARB — human story rebuild

## Goal
Turn `/about` into the page that introduces visitors to the people and story behind DARB. It should reduce the distance between a stranger and DARB through an honest founder story, a clear vision, and a warm team introduction — not explain the service workflow again.

## Page structure
1. **A more personal About hero**
   - Keep the established photo-banner style and stable hero sizing.
   - Replace process-focused copy with a short introduction to DARB as a student-founded organization.
   - Keep the hero free of buttons.

2. **How DARB began**
   - Lead with the true origin: DARB was started by a 22-year-old International Business student who is also an international student and had to learn German.
   - Tell the story in a grounded first-person founder note: facing the language, unfamiliar systems, and the need to understand the path personally.
   - Avoid invented dates, achievements, struggle stories, or dramatic founder mythology.

3. **Our story so far**
   - Replace the current “listen, organize, stay reachable” workflow timeline with a short story progression:
     - experiencing the journey as an international student;
     - recognizing the gap between information and practical understanding;
     - building DARB so other students do not have to begin as complete strangers to the process.
   - Use undated story chapters until genuine milestones are provided.

4. **Mission and vision**
   - Keep this section, but rewrite it around the human purpose rather than case management.
   - Mission: make international study feel understandable and less distant.
   - Vision: create a trusted bridge between students, the people guiding them, and the institutions involved.
   - Keep the language realistic and avoid guarantees or exaggerated impact claims.

5. **The team behind DARB**
   - Replace the current operational-principles section with a warm collective introduction to the team.
   - Explain that DARB brings together people who understand different parts of the student journey and work closely with students.
   - Do not show fake profiles, stock portraits, placeholder cards, invented roles, or emails.
   - Structure the section so verified portraits, names, roles, direct emails, and responsibilities can be added later without redesigning the page.

6. **A personal closing**
   - Add a restrained founder closing that speaks directly to visitors as people who may still be deciding whether to trust DARB.
   - Finish with the existing contact area so visitors can move naturally from learning who DARB is to starting a conversation.

## Visual direction
- Preserve the selected grounded editorial style, but make the page feel more human and biographical.
- Use the real office imagery already available; do not introduce stock team photography.
- Give the founder note visual emphasis through typography and spacing, not a fake portrait or oversized quotation marks.
- Use a simple story line and future-ready team layout, with restrained motion and no corporate icon grid.
- Preserve responsive LTR/RTL behavior and complete English, Arabic, and Hebrew copy.

## Content safeguards
- State only the founder details supplied: age 22, International Business student, international student, and learned German.
- Do not invent the founder’s name, founding year, team members, milestones, offices, metrics, partnerships, testimonials, or personal experiences beyond those facts.
- Keep public language-school claims hidden.
- Keep titles free of terminal punctuation.

## Verification
- Review the complete story for a natural, personal tone in English, Arabic, and Hebrew.
- Capture phone, tablet, laptop, and wide-desktop screenshots in all three languages.
- Check hero fit, founder-note readability, story flow, RTL ordering, team-section readiness, and horizontal overflow.
- Run localization, title-punctuation, and build checks.
